I prompted an AI to review TV shows or movies impersonating Elon Musk, Jordan Peterson, DHH, Paul Graham and other celebrities. It is funny and scary how AI nails the style and tone of these guys. I can literally hear the voice of the person it imitates.

# Better Call Saul - Pilot Episode
## Style: DHH (David Heinemeier Hansson)

> This is what happens when you optimize for the wrong metric. Jimmy McGill starts with a fundamental choice: Will he do the work properly, or will he chase the shortcut? He's a lawyer with potential—competent, quick-thinking, knows the game. But he's broke, working out of a nail salon, taking public defender cases for $700 each. Small, sustainable, honest. Then he sees the Kettlemans. Real money. A real opportunity. And instead of building his practice deliberately, he decides to manufacture it. He hires con artists to stage an accident. He's going to hack his way to success.**
> 
> What's brilliant about this pilot is that it doesn't judge him for it—it just shows the *inevitable* consequences. His shortcut doesn't cut anything short; it just multiplies complexity. The con backfires. He ends up at gunpoint. And that opening scene in Omaha? That's the real bill coming due. He did optimize for something—he optimized for avoiding constraints instead of working within them. Now he's managing a Cinnabon, watching old commercials alone, unable to even be himself. The system he tried to game ended up grinding him down instead.**
> 
> The lesson isn't "crime doesn't pay." It's that there are no shortcuts to anything worth doing. Build what lasts or don't build at all.**
